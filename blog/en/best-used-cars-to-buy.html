What are the best used cars to buy? Criteria, reliability reports and a checklist

There is no single best used car: pick a model that fits your budget and use, then check what published reliability reports say about it and what the history of the actual car shows. This guide is for private buyers and for dealers sourcing stock. We quote reliability only from reports that have a name and a date. As of October 2026.

Why there is no ranking of best models here

We do not publish a ranking of the best used cars, because a ranking without a source cannot be checked. The reliability reports measure different things: the TÜV Report defects found at inspection in Germany, ADAC roadside call-outs, Which? what owners report in the United Kingdom. So we do not merge them into one league table; we show each with its name, date and limits.

Which reliability reports exist and what they measure

We read three reports for this article: the Which? survey (United Kingdom, 63,334 drivers), the TÜV Report 2026 (Germany, 9.5 million inspections, overall defect rate 21.5%) and the ADAC Pannenstatistik 2026 (158 models from 27 manufacturers, almost half of all cases caused by the 12-volt battery). The reports measure different things, so they cannot be compared directly.

Which models lead the TÜV Report 2026 for 2 to 3 year old cars

According to TÜV Rheinland (20 November 2025), the Mazda 2 is the overall winner among 2 to 3 year old cars on the German market. The class leaders are the Fiat 500E (mini), the BMW 1 and 2 Series (compact), the Mercedes-Benz C-Class (mid-size), the Mercedes-Benz B-Class (van-type MPV) and the Volkswagen T-Roc (SUV).

Six criteria for your own shortlist

When you choose a used car, check six things: the reliability of the model and year in a named, dated report, the history of the individual car (service book, number of owners, chassis number), the 12-volt battery, running costs, parts availability and, for electric and hybrid cars, a battery health report with the battery warranty. The first is about the model; the rest are about the individual car and its costs.

A checklist for the actual car

Check the individual car in six steps, from the cheapest check to the most expensive: a report on the model, the service book and invoices, a comparison of the chassis number, a battery test with fault codes read through OBD, a test drive that includes a stretch of fast road, and an independent inspection before you pay and sign the contract.

  1. Find a report for the model, year and engine and note the weak point it names.
  2. Ask for the service book and invoices and compare them with the mileage in the advert.
  3. Compare the chassis number in the papers with the one on the car.
  4. Have the battery tested and read stored fault codes with an on-board diagnostics (OBD) reader. It reads faults; it is not proof of mileage.
  5. Take a test drive that includes at least one stretch of fast road, and check the brakes.
  6. Book an independent inspection before you pay, then sign the contract.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most reliable used car?

There is no single answer, because the reports measure different things and apply to a given market, model and age. For cars aged 2 to 3 years, TÜV Rheinland names the Mazda 2 as overall winner of the TÜV Report 2026, but that is not a ranking for every model year.

Are electric cars more reliable than petrol or diesel cars?

The reports we read do not allow a general answer. Compare by model and year, and for an electric car ask for a battery health report and the warranty terms.

Should I trust a ranking in an advert or a blog?

Only if it names the report, the period and the market. A ranking without those details is an opinion.

Which matters more, the brand or the service history?

In our view, the service history, because it relates to your car. Reports on brands and models help you rule out models with known problems but do not assess an individual vehicle.
