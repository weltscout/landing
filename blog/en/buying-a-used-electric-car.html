Buying a used electric car: what to check before you pay

A used electric car is a sound purchase when you can see the battery's state of health, the paperwork and the charging connectors before you pay. This guide puts the checks in the order you can do them and shows which EU rules help you and which do not yet reach cars on sale today. As of October 2026.

Ask for the battery's state of health and for the method behind the number

A state-of-health figure is only useful when you know how it was measured. The EU Battery Regulation defines state of health as a measure of the general condition of a rechargeable battery and its ability to deliver the specified performance compared with its initial condition (Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, Article 3). The Euro 7 regulation adds that the term is not commonly defined and is determined by different methods, for example "state of certified energy" and "state of certified range" (Regulation (EU) 2024/1257, recital 33). Two reports with the same percentage can therefore rest on different measurements.

Ask three things: which method produced the number, when it was measured, and who measured it. The answer can come from the car's own display, from the manufacturer or from an independent workshop.

Three EU rules shape what you can ask for, and none of them guarantees a used car a particular battery condition.

Warranty: the legal guarantee and the battery warranty are two different things

When a consumer buys from a trader, the seller is liable for defects that exist at delivery and appear within two years. For second-hand goods a Member State may allow the parties to agree a shorter period, but not shorter than one year (Directive (EU) 2019/771, Article 10). A defect that appears within the first year is presumed to have existed at delivery unless the seller proves otherwise, and Member States may extend this to two years (Article 11). A private sale is a different case, so ask first who the seller is.

The manufacturer's battery warranty is a separate contract. Before you pay, get these points in writing:

  • how many years and kilometres it covers;
  • the minimum battery capacity it promises, and how that is measured;
  • whether it passes to a second owner;
  • whether you must service the car at authorised workshops to keep it.

Charging: check the inlet, the power and one real charge

Public charging points in the EU must at least offer Type 2 connectors for AC and Combo 2 (CCS) connectors for DC (Regulation (EU) 2023/1804, Annex II), so a car with a Type 2 inlet and, for fast charging, the Combo 2 variant fits those points. Check the inlet on the car, then read the maximum AC and DC charging power in the specification sheet. Charge the car once on AC and, if you can, once on a DC point before you agree to the price.

Questions about used electric cars

Which battery state of health is acceptable?

No EU rule sets a minimum for used cars. As a reference, Euro 7 sets 80 % up to 5 years or 100,000 km for new battery-electric cars, but only from November 2026 for new types. Compare the figure with the car's age and mileage and ask how it was measured.

Does the legal guarantee apply when I buy from a private person?

The EU directive on sales of goods covers contracts between a consumer and a trader. For a private sale, national law applies, so check it for your country before you sign.

Is there a subsidy for a used electric car?

It depends on the country and the programme. Germany's federal programme for 2026 requires a new car first registered in Germany from 1 January 2026 (BAFA). Slovenia's call JP SUB-EV26 accepts used electric cars up to three years old, bought from a registered seller and not from a private person (Borzen FAQ). Check the status of a call before you rely on it.

Can I read the battery data myself?

The Battery Regulation gives the person who legally purchased the battery read-only access to the state-of-health data in the battery management system (Article 14). In practice you need the manufacturer's tool or a workshop that can read it, so ask the seller to arrange this before you buy.
