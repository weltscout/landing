Do you resell or import cars? A sourcing routine for dealers

You find better stock when you fix your buying criteria in writing, watch the markets that matter on a schedule, and settle the questions on VAT, registration and paperwork before you bid. This guide is for dealers and brokers who resell locally or import from other countries. It gives no tax or duty amounts. As of October 2026.

Start with a written buying profile

A buying profile, with one sheet per customer or per stock target, decides what you look for before you start browsing.

  • Make, model and generation, and which versions you will not take.
  • Year and mileage range.
  • The highest purchase price at which you still make your margin. Work it out from your own costs and margin.
  • Must-have equipment, fuel type and gearbox.
  • Seller type: dealer or private. It affects VAT and the paperwork (see below).
  • Markets: which countries you are willing to buy in.
  • Whether you accept damaged cars, and how far you will transport one.

What to clarify before you bid on a car from abroad

Settle these seven points before you bid, because a missing answer on any of them can turn a good price into a bad deal.

  1. Is the seller a dealer or a private person? The VAT treatment differs (Your Europe, read on 19 October 2026).
  2. Will the invoice show VAT separately or not? Ask in writing before you travel.
  3. Does the seller hold the original registration certificate, with all its parts? Your Europe says you should be able to get all the documents you need to register the car, in the original (read on 19 October 2026).
  4. What does the country where you will register the car require? It can ask for extra documents, such as proof of VAT payment (Your Europe, read on 19 October 2026).
  5. Who inspects the car, and when? Consider an independent inspection before you commit.
  6. How is the car transported, and who insures it on the way?
  7. Who is your buyer, and does your margin still work with the real costs?

Frequently asked questions

How often should I check for new listings?

As often as you can act on what you find. WeltScout checks once or twice a day, depending on the plan, so it fits routines where you review matches once or twice a day.

Do I pay VAT differently when I buy from a private seller abroad?

Your Europe says that a private person who buys from a private seller does not pay VAT in the seller's country and pays VAT only in the country where the car is registered (read on 19 October 2026). The page covers private buyers, so ask your accountant how it applies to your company.

Can I set up a search for my own stock?

Yes. A search does not have to be linked to a customer, and new listings reach you just as they do for customers.
