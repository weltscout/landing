How to find a used car online faster, step by step

You can speed up a used-car search with three things: clear criteria, saved searches with alerts and one shared notes sheet. The article is for dealers sourcing cars for customers and for buyers searching for themselves. As of October 2026.

Write the criteria down first

Speed starts with criteria: if you know what you do not want, you can reject a listing faster. Sort your criteria into three groups and decide where each one lives in the search.

GroupExamplesHow to use it
Must haveMake and model, maximum price, maximum mileage, fuel typeSet as filters on the portal
Nice to haveColour, trim pack, panoramic roofDo not filter, or you will hide good cars too; mark them in your notes and compare the finalists
Ruled outNo service book, damaged vehicle, unclear historyWrite it down in advance and stick to it; if the listing does not say, ask the seller

Save your searches and switch on alerts

Many larger portals offer saved searches with alerts; whether and how is something to check in each portal's settings. Instead of retyping your conditions every day, let new listings come to you.

Set up two versions of the same search: a narrow one that matches your criteria exactly and a wider one with a slightly higher price or more mileage. The narrow one tells you what to buy, and the wide one shows whether your prices are close to the market.

Weed out duplicate listings

The same car is sometimes listed more than once, by different sellers or on several portals. Copy the VIN into your sheet when you first view a listing: if it turns up again, it is a duplicate and you can drop it. Check the vehicle history by VIN before you phone the seller.

If a portal lets you hide sellers, hide those who post incomplete details or photos that do not match the description.

Keep one shared notes sheet

One sheet for all portals shows what you have already checked. Use a spreadsheet with these columns.

ColumnWhat it is for
Link and portalGetting back to the listing quickly
Price, year, mileageComparing finalists
VINSpotting duplicates and checking history
Date first seenTelling new listings from old ones
Status (called, rejected, viewing)Not phoning the same seller twice
NoteNice to have: colour, equipment; ruled out: reason for rejecting

A quick listing check in six questions

  1. Do year, mileage and equipment match the description and the photos?
  2. Are there photos from every side, including the interior and the engine bay?
  3. Is the VIN given, or does the seller send it when asked?
  4. Is the price within the range you expect compared with similar listings?
  5. Does the listing avoid everything in your ruled-out group?
  6. Does the seller answer questions?

Official sources for buying in another EU country

If you find a listing in another EU country, settle VAT and vehicle registration before you buy. The official pages Buying, selling and leasing a car in another EU country and Registration of cars and motorised vehicles in the EU explain the rules (read on 19 October 2026; the EU last checked the first page on 21 April 2026). We do not quote duty amounts because they depend on the vehicle and the country.

Questions and answers

How often should I check for new listings?

At every alert, if you receive them. Without alerts, pick a fixed time, for instance a morning check, so you do not miss listings that have been up for a day or two.

Are a portal's saved searches enough?

For one portal, yes. If you search on several portals or in several countries, you have to set up and maintain each one separately.
