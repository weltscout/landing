Import a used car from Germany to Serbia: customs, VAT, Euro 3 and registration

A used car from Germany enters Serbia with 0% customs duty if you give customs proof of EU origin, either an EUR.1 certificate or an invoice declaration for cars worth up to 6,000 euros, and VAT is 20%. The car must meet the Euro 3 standard, and clearance always goes through the regular procedure. The duty rate without proof of origin comes from the Customs Tariff, which we did not read, so we state no figure. To register the car you need proof that customs and tax are settled, a roadworthiness certificate at most 30 days old and insurance. Status: 28 October 2026.

What you pay

The Serbian Customs Administration states that for cars from EU member states the duty rate is 0% if the importer gives customs proof of origin, an EUR.1 certificate or, for cars up to 6,000 euros, an invoice declaration; VAT is 20% (Uprava carina, page "Sporazumi", read on 28 October 2026). The VAT base is the value determined under customs rules plus customs duty, other import charges and incidental costs up to the first destination in Serbia (VAT Act, Article 19).

Customs checks the value against the invoice or sales contract that goes with the declaration. Secondary sources quote a 12.5% duty without proof of origin; we could not find that rate in the official text we read, so check the row for heading 8703 in the current Customs Tariff or ask the customs office.

Which cars can be imported

A car can be imported if it was made to the Euro 3 standard; Uprava carina treats cars made after 1 January 2002 and first registered in the EU or EFTA as meeting it, and the standard cannot be reached by later modification (Decree on the import of motor vehicles, Article 4; Uprava carina, act of 20 February 2023). A used vehicle is one that was registered and whose first registration is at least six months old. It does not have to be deregistered abroad and can enter on regular or temporary plates; it is cleared in the regular procedure only, never in the short procedure at the border.

If the registration certificate stays in the country of deregistration, the copy must be certified by a competent authority abroad (court, notary, police, municipality); a certification by a sales agency or an automobile club is not accepted (Uprava carina, act of 20 February 2023). For registration the Ministry of the Interior's rules list a roadworthiness certificate not older than 30 days, compulsory liability insurance, proof that customs and tax obligations are settled, proof of payment of the registration costs, proof of ownership or origin and proof of identity (Rulebook on registration, Article 5).

Plates for the drive from Germany

You need plates and insurance valid in every country on your route; temporary plates are not harmonised in the EU (Your Europe). A German export plate is valid for the period of the insurance, at most one year (§ 45 FZV), a short-term plate until the end of the fifth day after issue (§ 42 FZV). As one example the Berlin office charges at least 33.20 euros plus the plates (Service Berlin).

What this means for buyers and dealers

For a private buyer

Ask the seller for an EUR.1 or an invoice declaration before you pay: without proof of origin the duty is not 0%. Keep the original registration certificate, or its certified copy, in the file.

For a dealer

A private person imports for own needs only (Uprava carina); import for resale needs different conditions, so check them at the customs office. Frequently asked questions

How much customs duty is due on a used car from Germany?

0% with proof of EU origin (EUR.1, or an invoice declaration for cars up to 6,000 euros) and VAT of 20% (Uprava carina). Without proof, the rate comes from the Customs Tariff, which we did not read.

Do I need an EUR.1?

Yes, for the 0% rate: the proof of origin is attached to the declaration. For cars worth up to 6,000 euros an invoice declaration is enough.

Which emission standard must the car meet?

Euro 3, and it cannot be reached by later modification. Cars first registered in the EU after 1 January 2002 are treated as meeting it.

Can I import a car that is still registered in Germany?

Yes. It need not be deregistered abroad, but it is declared at the border, and cars on temporary plates need a transit declaration.
