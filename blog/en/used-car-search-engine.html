A five-step test

The test takes two days of noting listings by hand.

  1. Define three searches: model, budget and country.
  2. For two days, note new listings by hand on two portals.
  3. Compare your list with what the tool showed.
  4. Count the listings the tool missed and those it found before you did.
  5. Read the terms: minimum term, cancellation and price with or without VAT.

Tax and registration when buying abroad: the official source

A tool finds the listing; tax and registration follow official rules. According to Your Europe (page last checked 21 September 2026), when you buy a used car from a dealer, VAT may or may not be charged and is shown separately on the invoice, while a private seller is treated differently. You register a used car in the country where you permanently live and pay registration and road tax there. We state no amounts. Check the official Your Europe page on VAT when buying or selling a car abroad and your own tax authority.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a search tool if I already use a portal?

Not necessarily. A portal shows its own listings. A search tool helps if you look across several portals or countries and do not want to check each by hand.

How many listings does a search tool really cover?

You cannot tell from a description, so run the test in this article. If a provider does not state its coverage, treat that as a warning sign.

Will a search tool buy the car for me?

No. It finds the listing and alerts you. Inspecting the car, negotiating, the contract, tax and registration are yours to handle.
