Used car sourcing for dealers: channels, process and tracking

Dealers source used cars in two ways: stock they buy at their own risk, and a car bought to a customer's brief. The process is the same for both: criteria, search across several sources, a quick margin check, verification, and only then the purchase. This guide is for dealers and brokers. As of October 2026.

Two kinds of sourcing: stock and customer brief

You buy stock because you expect to sell it, and a car to a brief because a customer has already asked for it. The difference shows up in your criteria and in your risk.

StockTo a customer's brief
Who sets the criteriaYou, from demand and your own salesThe customer; you write them down and confirm
Main riskThe car stays unsoldThe customer drops out or buys elsewhere during the search
What makes it easierA saved search for the models that sell for youA written brief and a separate search for each customer

The second kind is what many people call vehicle sourcing, and it is the same work in practice: finding, checking, buying and handing over a car that matches the customer's criteria.

The sourcing process, step by step

The process pays off when you run every offer through the same steps in the same order.

VAT when buying a used car in another EU country

Whether you pay VAT when buying in another EU country depends on how the seller accounts for it, so check the invoice before you commit. Your Europe, which we read on 19 October 2026, says that when you buy a used car from a dealer, the VAT may or may not be charged and mentioned separately on the invoice, depending on how the dealer opts to calculate VAT on that car.

Your own VAT and registration duties depend on the country where you are established, and the rules for traders differ from those for private buyers. Check the guidance of your national tax authority or ask a tax adviser before the first cross-border purchase. We do not give tax amounts here. The official starting point is Value added tax (VAT) when buying or selling a car abroad.

What this means for a dealer

  • Write down the maximum purchase price for each car before you start searching.
  • For every foreign offer, check the seller's tax treatment on the invoice before you buy.
  • Record the source and outcome of every purchase, so in a few months you know which channel pays.
  • If you search for several customers at once, keep a separate search for each, so none is neglected.

Frequently asked questions

What is vehicle sourcing?

It is finding, checking and buying a car that matches a customer's criteria, usually for an agreed fee. Write down the criteria and the scope of the service before you start searching.
