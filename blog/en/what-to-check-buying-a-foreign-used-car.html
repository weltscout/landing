What to check when buying a foreign used car: a practical guide

A used car from abroad is worth buying only if the price gap survives all the costs. So work out the total cost first, then choose the country and the seller, and only then go to see cars. This guide is for buyers and dealers in EU countries, with a note on Great Britain. As of October 2026.

Dealer or private seller: what changes

Checks before you transfer money

Send money only after every step, because fixing a mistake after payment costs the most.

  1. Get the chassis number and photos of the original documents.
  2. Check the vehicle history with an independent service, and compare the mileage with the service book.
  3. View the car yourself, or have an independent expert inspect it where it stands if it is far away.
  4. Make sure the car comes with the documents you need to register it in your own country.
  5. Put the vehicle details, seller, price and any condition the seller guarantees into the contract.
  6. Pay on collection or through a secure channel, never in advance without safeguards.

Transport and temporary plates

Temporary plates are not harmonised across the EU, so another country may not accept them. Your Europe describes a car bought in Germany that was driven through Hungary on temporary plates valid only in Germany, which led to a fine and the car being confiscated. Before you set off, ask the authority in the country of purchase where your plates will be valid.

Registration at home

You register the car in the country where you live, and Your Europe notes there are no common EU rules on vehicle registration and related taxes, so use your national authority. Your Europe says the permanent plate should come from the country where you register the car, which is usually where you live. If you are bringing the car into Great Britain, GOV.UK says to tell HMRC within 14 days of arrival, make an import declaration if HMRC says duty and VAT are due, and register and tax the car with the DVLA after vehicle approval.

What this means for a buyer

  • Fill in the worksheet for every listing, then plan the trip.
  • If the seller is private, count on having no guarantee under EU consumer rules.
  • Keep the invoice and the original documents, because you need them to register the car.

What this means for a dealer

Dealers often buy abroad for resale. When you buy from a dealer in another EU country, check the VAT treatment on the invoice, because the standard rules and the used-goods margin scheme are handled differently. Frequently asked questions

Is it really cheaper to buy a used car abroad?

Sometimes, but there is no general rule. Only the total cost for a specific car answers it, so fill in the worksheet.

Can I drive a car home on temporary plates?

It depends on the country of purchase. Your Europe warns that temporary plates are not harmonised across the EU and another country may not recognise them, so ask beforehand.

How much guarantee do I get when buying in another EU country?

A minimum of 2 years when you buy from a professional seller, which can be reduced to no less than 1 year for used cars if agreed at purchase. EU consumer rights do not apply to private sales (Your Europe, read on 19 October 2026).

Do I pay VAT in the seller's country?

On a used car from a private seller you generally do not. From a dealer it depends on how the dealer calculates VAT on that car, so look at the invoice.
